# Product Lifecycle — Capability Stages

These are **business maturity stages**, not software version numbers or fixed deadlines. Stages may overlap. Advancing requires evidence, appropriate resources, and legal review where relevant.

## Stage 1 — Independent investor tools (current strategic focus)
**Users:** individual investors. **Core principle:** no broker agreement required.

Supported document imports and available manual workflows let investors organize holdings, transactions, coupons, dividends, and fees. The actual implemented feature set must be verified in the KMP/backend repositories; see [Features](FEATURES.md).

**Website:** clearly explain independence, show verified features and screenshots, offer educational content, and answer common questions.

**Gate to next stage:** reliable calculations, acceptable import accuracy, strong privacy/security, and evidence that external users find value.

## Stage 2 — Public product and validation
Release the mobile app through approved stores when ready. Expand supported formats based on user requests, improve onboarding, gather feedback, and test modest acquisition channels.

**Gate:** meaningful user retention and manageable support/operating costs. Do not treat downloads alone as proof of demand.

## Stage 3 — Optional broker-connected experience
Where a broker agrees and the customer authorizes it, introduce scoped, secure account synchronization. **Users of non-partner brokers must still have a viable independent workflow.**

**Gate:** signed agreements, validated consent and legal basis, secure integration, partner operational readiness, and proven customer value.

## Stage 4 — Institutional software provider
Offer validated broker-facing products, potentially document ingestion, reporting, reconciliation, and APIs. Avoid building a complete broker CRM or back-office before discovering actual workflows and winning pilots.

**Gate:** repeatable paid institutional demand, support capacity, security controls, and sustainable unit economics.

## Stage 5 — Optional regulated brokerage
A possible **separate strategic decision**, not a promised product feature. Would require verified licensing, capital, governance, qualified personnel, operational controls, and commercial justification. Staying a software provider may be preferable.

## Rules across every stage
- No fabricated partnerships or approvals.
- No automatic access to broker customer data.
- No requirement that every user have a participating broker.
- Preserve transparent, auditable financial records.
- Defer capabilities until validation and explicit approval.

## Website implications
The website should present **Stage 1 verified capabilities as current**, describe other stages as exploratory future directions where useful, and never market speculative services as available.
