## Start with the denominator
A fixed coupon rate describes annual interest relative to face value. Current yield compares annual coupon income with the bond’s current price. They answer different questions and need not be equal.

Yield to maturity is a broader calculated measure based on price and the scheduled cash flows through maturity. It depends on assumptions, including payments being made as promised; realizing that return also depends on reinvestment conditions. It is not a guaranteed outcome. The [SEC’s bond bulletin](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/what-are) explains the distinctions in general terms.

## Compare like with like
When reading a quote, ask which yield is shown, when the price was observed, and whether the calculation includes costs or taxes. A coupon percentage alone is not enough to compare investments bought at different prices.

## Label your records clearly
Keep separate fields or notes for face value, purchase price, coupon rate, costs and the date of any valuation. Do not relabel coupon income as total investment performance.
