## Keep a path back to the evidence
Good records help explain where a number came from. [Investor.gov’s recordkeeping guidance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/broker-dealers-record-keeping-requirements) encourages investors to keep statements and confirmations. Its US broker retention rules do not determine how long a Rwanda investor must retain documents.

The following is an editorial organizing routine, not a legal retention schedule.

## A repeatable review
1. Keep an original copy of each transaction, allocation and payment record in storage you control.
2. Identify the broker, investment, date and currency so similar records are distinguishable.
3. Review imported or manually entered values against that evidence.
4. Compare holdings and confirmed income with statements; leave discrepancies flagged until explained.
5. Note corrections so you can understand later why a record changed.

Avoid sending account numbers or private investment PDFs through a public website contact form.

## Use tracking as an aid
Struct Capital supports organizing investment records, transaction history and reports. Supporting a document format does not require a broker partnership. An app summary or exported report should be reconciled with original records; it is not a substitute for official statements, independent tax advice or an audit.
